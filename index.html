Overview

Three pillars, one community-engaged core.

CHIP joins environmental epidemiology, community-engaged research, implementation science, and applied evaluation. Our work is organized around three research pillars that share a common community-engaged core and a unified methodological toolkit. Rather than operating separately, the pillars overlap: environmental exposures drive cardiometabolic risk, food environments shape metabolic health, and occupational conditions cut across all three — allowing us to study cumulative, place-based risk and respond with the communities most affected.

Research

From exposure measurement to community action.

Our research program spans three connected pillars, each pairing field research with the methods and partnerships that turn findings into practice.

Cardiometabolic & chronic disease

Examining the drivers of cardiometabolic and chronic disease risk in border Latino communities, and delivering field-based screening that turns measurement into action.

Community cardiometabolic screening

Bilingual biometric screening in colonias, schools, and health fairs, with education and clinical referral.

Cohort & biomarker research

Longitudinal analysis of metabolic syndrome and cardiovascular risk across the life course.

School physical activity & wellness

Wellness and physical-activity programming supporting cardiometabolic health.

Environmental & occupational health

Studying how traffic-related air pollution, extreme heat, and occupational conditions contribute to cardiorespiratory and cardiometabolic health in communities affected by industrial and cross-border activity.

Border exposure assessment

Community-engaged risk assessment near the Bridge of the Americas, with air, soil, and water sampling.

Geospatial exposure mapping

Satellite data, air-quality monitoring, and spatial epidemiology, including de-identified records for asthma and respiratory illness.

Occupational heat & ozone

Wearable monitoring and tiered measurement of combined heat and ozone exposure among outdoor workers.

Food systems & nutrition

School nutrition & meals research

Director

Juan Aguilera, MD, PhD, MPH

Director, Community Health Impact Projects · Assistant Professor, UTHealth Houston School of Public Health in El Paso

Dr. Aguilera is a public health researcher whose work examines how environmental exposures, occupational conditions, and social determinants contribute to chronic disease risk in underserved populations, with a focus on the U.S.–Mexico border region. His program integrates environmental epidemiology, community-engaged research, implementation science, and applied program evaluation.
