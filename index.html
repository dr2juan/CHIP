Overview

Three pillars, one community-engaged core.

CHIP joins environmental epidemiology, community-engaged research, implementation science, and applied evaluation. Our work is organized around three research pillars that share a common community-engaged core and a unified methodological toolkit. Rather than operating separately, the pillars overlap: environmental exposures drive cardiometabolic risk, food environments shape metabolic health, and occupational conditions cut across all three — allowing us to study cumulative, place-based risk and respond with the communities most affected.

Featured study · Now in the field

The Air & Heat Study

Our flagship community survey — assessing the health impacts of extreme heat and air pollution in at-risk populations across El Paso.

The community survey · Encuesta comunitaria

What border residents experience — in their own words, in both languages.

An IRB-approved bilingual questionnaire on sociodemographics, healthcare access, chronic conditions, respiratory symptoms, air-pollution perceptions, and lived experience of heat waves — administered with on-site screenings in colonias, schools, and health fairs, and paired with NASA satellite exposure estimates. More than 400 residents screened and roughly 300 swabs biobanked to date.

From data to action

What changes because CHIP is in the field.

Measurement is the starting point, not the product. Each CHIP activity is designed so that data collected with a community returns to that community as something usable — a result, a referral, a baseline, or a shared map.

Screening → Referral

Results leave the field with the participant.

Community screenings return biometric results on the spot, in English or Spanish, with plain-language education. Participants with elevated readings are connected to local clinical partners for follow-up — so a screening event is a doorway into care, not just a data point.

Baseline → Accountability

A before-and-after record for a changing border.

Biospecimens and environmental samples collected near the Bridge of the Americas before commercial cargo diversion give neighboring communities a scientific baseline — a reference point for evaluating whether health and exposure conditions actually change after the port does.

Assessment → Shared map

One inventory the whole region can plan around.

The regional healthy-eating needs assessment, developed with the Paso del Norte Health Foundation, documents 204 nutrition programs in one place — built so funders, coalitions, and providers can target gaps instead of duplicating effort.

Partner with CHIP

One platform, three ways to work together.

CHIP was built on the U.S.–Mexico border, and the platform travels: bilingual field teams, screening infrastructure, exposure science, and evaluation methodology that can be applied wherever they are needed. All engagements are set up through UTHealth Houston — contact us and we will walk you through the details.

Grant collaboration

We strengthen your proposal

As Co-Investigator, MPI, or subaward site, CHIP arrives with assets most proposals spend a cycle building: pilot data from an established border cohort, exposure assessment and geospatial cores, community engagement infrastructure, and a field team ready for data collection from day one.

Contracted services

A turnkey field & evaluation team

Program evaluation, community needs assessments, bilingual screening events, exposure and geospatial analysis, survey design and data collection — scoped, delivered, and reported. Contracting runs through UTHealth Houston, and we make the setup simple.

Implementation partnerships

Bring the model to your region

Deploy the CHIP model — community health worker–delivered screening with education and clinical referral — in your county, health system, or service area. We train, adapt, and evaluate alongside your local team.

Start a conversation

Students and trainees: field research, evaluation, and analysis opportunities are available within the program — reach out by email.

What we bring to your proposal
  • An established, IRB-experienced bilingual field team embedded in the community.
  • Pilot data: a ~5,000-person geocoded cohort, biobanked specimens, and validated exposure models.
  • A screening operation that doubles as a consented specimen-collection pipeline for epigenetic and biomarker studies.
  • Ready-to-deploy bilingual, IRB-approved instruments and evaluation designs.
  • A track record executing NIH, USDA, NIOSH, state, and foundation awards.
  • Letters of support and community partnerships on a grant timeline.
What one CHIP encounter captures

A single field encounter can collect data across the domains a proposal needs:

Sociodemographics & SDOH Healthcare access & utilization Chronic disease & family history Preventive screening adherence Respiratory health Air pollution exposure Heat exposure & vulnerability Biometrics — BP, glucose, lipids, anthropometrics Biospecimens for epigenetics Geocoded exposure linkage

New modules — like our air pollution and extreme heat instruments — extend the same bilingual questionnaire platform used in the founding ~5,000-participant cohort, so every new study stays comparable to a decade of baseline data.
