# DAIRY-HER — One-Page Pre-Proposal Brief

**Call:** National Dairy Council — Precision Nutrition in Women's Health (N) 2026
**Title:** Dairy Within Dietary Patterns Across the Menopause Transition: A Biomarker- and Phenotype-Driven Precision Nutrition Study in Border Latina Women (*DAIRY-HER*)
**PI:** Juan Aguilera, MD, PhD, MPH — Director, Community Health Impact Projects (CHIP); Assistant Professor, UTHealth Houston School of Public Health, El Paso · juan.aguilera@uth.tmc.edu
**Priority areas:** Both (biomarker/phenotype prediction of dairy response across menopausal stages; development/validation of a precision dairy-inclusive strategy).

**Problem.** The menopause transition compresses a decade of excess cardiometabolic risk — dyslipidemia, visceral adiposity, dysglycemia, rising blood pressure, inflammation, and muscle/bone loss — into a short window, yet nutrition guidance stays one-size-fits-all. Precision nutrition could tailor it, but its evidence base excludes the population with the highest need: Latina women. In CHIP's border cohort, measured metabolic syndrome prevalence is **37.3%**.

**Opportunity.** Dairy foods — high-quality protein, calcium, potassium, vitamin D, bioactive peptides, and (fermented) live cultures — are a plausible, culturally-authentic lever (*queso fresco*, yogurt, milk in *café*/*atole*). Average dairy–cardiometabolic effects are heterogeneous; that heterogeneity is the precision-nutrition opportunity. We study dairy **within overall dietary patterns** (matrix approach), not as head-to-head dairy comparisons — per the call's guidance.

**Central hypothesis.** Cardiometabolic response to dairy-inclusive patterns varies by menopausal stage and is predictable from an integrated phenotype (hormonal status, metabolomic signature, gut microbiome, genetic variants), so phenotype-guided dairy-inclusive guidance will outperform standard guidance.

**Aims.**
1. **Characterize & phenotype** — In pre-, peri-, and postmenopausal Latina women (~300–360), relate dairy-inclusive dietary patterns to cardiometabolic risk (lipids, BP, glycemia, inflammation) and identify the hormonal, metabolomic, microbiome, and genetic biomarkers predicting interindividual response.
2. **Model & validate** — Integrate multi-omic + phenotype data via machine learning into a validated response-prediction model; derive a minimal, field-deployable biomarker panel.
3. **Pilot & validate a precision strategy** — In a phenotype-stratified pilot (~60–90), test culturally-tailored, phenotype-guided dairy-inclusive guidance vs. standard guidance on cardiometabolic and musculoskeletal (grip strength, physical function) outcomes.

**Why CHIP (feasibility).** Paso del Norte cohort (n≈4,959; 37.3% MetS) as sampling frame; an operational bilingual **CHW screening network** (400+ screened since 2025) for recruitment/retention/field delivery; a working **biospecimen pipeline** (250+ specimens collected); active nutrition-evaluation portfolio (GusNIP produce-prescription, school nutrition, regional needs assessment); UTHealth Houston SPH + Harvard T.H. Chan affiliation.

**Innovation.** Life-stage precision (STRAW+10 + FSH/estradiol); dairy-as-matrix within patterns; multi-dimensional response prediction; equity built into model derivation, not bolted on; community-embedded delivery infrastructure.

**Impact.** First phenotype-driven map of dairy-inclusive response across menopause in Latina women; a validated precision model + minimal panel; pilot evidence for phenotype-guided guidance; a competitive NIH R01 / full-trial follow-on. **~30 months; feasibility-scale budget to NDC ceiling.**
