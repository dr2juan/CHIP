## 1. Overview / Abstract (≈250 words)

The menopause transition is a period of rapid, adverse cardiometabolic change — dyslipidemia, visceral adiposity, dysglycemia, rising blood pressure, systemic inflammation, and accelerating bone and muscle loss — yet nutrition guidance for this life stage remains one-size-fits-all. Precision nutrition promises to tailor guidance to the individual, but its evidence base is built largely on well-resourced, non-Hispanic White cohorts. Latinas, who carry among the highest burdens of metabolic syndrome and enter menopause with distinct metabolic and sociocultural profiles, are almost entirely absent from this science.

We propose to close both gaps at once. Building on CHIP's Paso del Norte Community Health and Environment Cohort (Wave 1 n≈4,959; measured metabolic syndrome prevalence 37.3%) and an established bilingual community-health-worker (CHW) screening and biospecimen network, we will deeply phenotype pre-, peri-, and postmenopausal Latina women to determine **how dairy foods, embedded within overall dietary patterns, modulate cardiometabolic risk — and for whom.**

**Aim 1** characterizes associations between dairy-inclusive dietary patterns and cardiometabolic risk factors across menopausal stages, and identifies the hormonal, metabolomic, gut-microbiome, and genetic signatures that predict interindividual response. **Aim 2** integrates these multi-dimensional data into a validated precision model that classifies women by predicted cardiometabolic response to dairy-inclusive patterns. **Aim 3** pilots and validates a culturally-tailored, phenotype-guided dairy-inclusive dietary strategy against standard guidance, testing cardiometabolic and musculoskeletal outcomes.

The result: actionable, equity-centered precision-nutrition guidance for the menopause transition — generated in, and returned to, a high-burden population that current science overlooks.

## 2. Background and Significance

**The menopause transition is a cardiometabolic inflection point.** Independent of chronological aging, the decline in ovarian estrogen across the peri- to postmenopausal transition drives a shift toward an atherogenic lipid profile (rising LDL-C, ApoB, and small dense LDL), central and visceral fat redistribution, reduced insulin sensitivity, higher blood pressure, and a rise in systemic inflammatory tone. In parallel, bone mineral density and lean muscle mass decline, elevating fracture and sarcopenia risk. These changes compress a decade of excess cardiometabolic and musculoskeletal risk into a relatively short window — making midlife a high-value, biologically-defined target for nutrition intervention.

**Dairy foods are a plausible, underused lever — but the evidence is heterogeneous.** Meta-analyses and cohort studies broadly report neutral-to-favorable associations between dairy intake and cardiometabolic outcomes, with fermented dairy (e.g., yogurt) most consistently linked to lower type 2 diabetes risk, and dairy contributing high-quality protein, calcium, potassium, magnesium, vitamin D (fortified), bioactive peptides, and — for fermented products — live cultures relevant to the gut microbiome. The emerging **"dairy matrix"** concept holds that the health effects of a dairy food arise from the integrated food structure, not from isolated nutrients, and that effects depend on the overall dietary pattern in which the food is consumed. Critically, the *heterogeneity* in these findings is itself the scientific opportunity: average effects mask substantial interindividual variability, and that variability is exactly what precision nutrition exists to explain.

**Interindividual variability is predictable — but has not been mapped in this population or life stage.** Landmark precision-nutrition work has shown that postprandial glycemic and lipemic responses to identical foods vary widely between people and are predictable from gut-microbiome composition, metabolomic state, anthropometrics, and genetics. Yet these models were derived largely in non-Hispanic, pre-menopausal or mixed populations, and none were designed around the menopause transition or around dairy-inclusive patterns. Genetic modifiers plausibly relevant to dairy response — including *LCT/MCM6* lactase-persistence variants (which differ in frequency in admixed Latino populations), *FADS1/2* (fatty-acid desaturation), and *APOE* (lipid response) — remain unstudied in this context.

**Border Latina women are a high-burden, high-priority, and understudied population.** The Paso del Norte region is predominantly Mexican-origin Latino, with a measured metabolic syndrome prevalence of 37.3% in CHIP's community cohort — well above national averages. Latinas experience earlier and more severe cardiometabolic risk yet are markedly underrepresented in precision-nutrition research, meaning that emerging personalized guidance risks being least accurate for those who could benefit most. Dairy is deeply embedded in the regional food culture (e.g., *queso fresco*, *crema*, yogurt, milk in *café* and *atole*), making dairy-inclusive dietary patterns both culturally authentic and practically modifiable.

**Significance.** This study generates the first biomarker- and phenotype-driven map of dairy-inclusive dietary response across the menopause transition in Latina women, and translates it into a validated, culturally-tailored precision strategy. It advances the National Dairy Council's priority of understanding how dairy contributes *within* dietary patterns, while extending precision nutrition to a population where the science — and the health need — is greatest.

## 3. Innovation

- **Life-stage precision.** Anchors dairy-response phenotyping to biologically-defined menopausal stage (STRAW+10 criteria plus measured FSH/estradiol), rather than treating midlife as a static covariate.
- **Matrix, not match-up.** Studies dairy foods as contributors within culturally-grounded dietary patterns — directly aligned with the call's guidance to avoid head-to-head comparisons of specific dairy types.
- **Multi-dimensional response prediction.** Integrates hormonal status, untargeted metabolomics, gut-microbiome profiling, and targeted genetics into a single machine-learning response model — moving from *whether* dairy helps to *for whom, at which life stage, and why.*
- **Equity as design, not afterthought.** Builds the precision model in a high-burden Latina population from the outset, countering the field's structural bias and producing guidance that generalizes to those most affected.
- **Community-embedded infrastructure.** Leverages an operational CHW screening and biospecimen network, enabling recruitment, retention, and real-world delivery that most precision-nutrition studies cannot achieve.

## 4. Central Hypothesis and Specific Aims

**Central hypothesis:** Cardiometabolic response to dairy-inclusive dietary patterns varies systematically across the menopause transition and is predictable from an integrated phenotype comprising hormonal status, metabolomic signature, gut-microbiome composition, and genetic variants — such that a precision, phenotype-guided dairy-inclusive strategy will improve cardiometabolic and musculoskeletal outcomes more than standard guidance.

**Specific Aim 1 — Characterize and phenotype.** In a cohort of pre-, peri-, and postmenopausal Latina women (leveraging the Paso del Norte cohort and CHW-network recruitment), determine the associations between dairy-inclusive dietary patterns and cardiometabolic risk factors (lipid profile, blood pressure, glycemic control, systemic inflammation), and identify the hormonal, metabolomic, gut-microbiome, and genetic biomarkers/phenotypes that predict interindividual variability in these associations across menopausal stages.

**Specific Aim 2 — Model and validate response prediction.** Integrate the multi-dimensional phenotype data using machine-learning methods to build and internally validate a model that classifies women by predicted cardiometabolic response to dairy-inclusive dietary patterns, and identify the minimal biomarker panel that retains predictive performance for field use.

**Specific Aim 3 — Pilot and validate a precision strategy.** In a pilot validation study, deliver a culturally-tailored, phenotype-guided dairy-inclusive dietary strategy versus standard menopause nutrition guidance, and test whether phenotype-matched guidance improves cardiometabolic (lipids, glycemia, blood pressure, inflammation) and musculoskeletal (grip strength, physical function) outcomes — validating the Aim 2 model's predictions.

## 5. Approach (Summary)

**Design & population.** Prospective deep-phenotyping cohort of ~300–360 Latina women aged ~40–65, stratified across pre-, peri-, and postmenopausal stages (STRAW+10 + FSH/estradiol), recruited via the Paso del Norte cohort and the CHW screening network. A phenotype-stratified subset (~60–90) enters the Aim 3 pilot validation (randomized, phenotype-guided vs. standard guidance; crossover or parallel with washout, to be finalized).

**Dairy exposure & dietary patterns (Aims 1–3).** Culturally-adapted, validated dietary assessment (multiple 24-hour recalls + FFQ) characterizing overall dietary patterns and the contribution of dairy foods within them (total, fermented, and by matrix/fat level as covariates — not as head-to-head arms). Objective dairy-intake biomarkers (e.g., odd-chain fatty acids C15:0/C17:0) to reduce self-report error.

**Cardiometabolic outcomes.** Fasting lipid panel (including ApoB where feasible), blood pressure, HbA1c and fasting glucose/insulin (HOMA-IR), and systemic inflammation (hs-CRP, IL-6). Leverages existing CHIP biometric-screening protocols.

**Predictive biomarkers/phenotypes.**
- *Hormonal status:* FSH, estradiol; menopausal staging.
- *Metabolomics:* untargeted plasma metabolomic signatures (lipidomics emphasis).
- *Gut microbiome:* stool 16S/shotgun profiling of composition and relevant functional pathways (SCFA, bile-acid, lactose metabolism).
- *Genetics:* targeted variants (*LCT/MCM6* lactase persistence, *FADS1/2*, *APOE*, plus a cardiometabolic panel).
- *Musculoskeletal (Aim 3):* grip strength and short physical performance battery; body composition where feasible.

**Analysis.**
- *Aim 1:* Mixed-effects and pattern-based models (e.g., reduced-rank regression) relating dairy-inclusive patterns to cardiometabolic outcomes, with menopausal stage and biomarker interactions; multiple-comparison control for -omics.
- *Aim 2:* Regularized/ensemble machine learning (e.g., elastic net, gradient boosting) integrating clinical, hormonal, metabolomic, microbiome, and genetic features; nested cross-validation; minimal-panel reduction; predefined performance metrics.
- *Aim 3:* Intention-to-treat comparison of cardiometabolic/musculoskeletal change by arm; test of whether observed response aligns with model-predicted response (calibration). Powered for feasibility and effect-size estimation.

**Human subjects & community engagement.** UTHealth Houston IRB oversight; bilingual consent; CHW-mediated recruitment and retention; community advisory input on dietary strategy design and results return.

## 6. Feasibility and Research Environment (Why CHIP)

CHIP offers infrastructure that de-risks every aim:

- **An established cohort with cardiometabolic phenotyping.** The Paso del Norte Community Health and Environment Cohort (Wave 1 n≈4,959; measured metabolic syndrome prevalence 37.3%) provides a sampling frame, prior measures, and demonstrated retention in exactly the target population.
- **An operational CHW screening network.** A trained bilingual community-health-worker team already delivers biometric screening in colonias, schools, and health fairs (400+ community members screened since early 2025), providing a proven recruitment, retention, and field-delivery engine.
- **A working biospecimen pipeline.** CHIP already collects and handles field biospecimens (e.g., 250+ buccal swabs collected near the port of entry), demonstrating capacity for the genetic, metabolomic, and microbiome sampling this study requires.
- **Food-systems and nutrition-evaluation expertise.** Active nutrition-incentive (produce-prescription/GusNIP) evaluation, school-nutrition research, and a regional healthy-eating needs assessment situate the team to design culturally-authentic dairy-inclusive dietary strategies.
- **Methodological depth.** The program integrates environmental epidemiology, community-engaged research, implementation science, geospatial analysis, and applied evaluation — with the analytic capacity for multi-omic, machine-learning response modeling.
- **Institutional strength.** UTHealth Houston School of Public Health (El Paso campus) with affiliation to Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health (Environmental Health), and a track record of federal and foundation funding (NIH/NIMHD, USDA/NIFA/GusNIP, NIOSH-SWCOEH, American Society for Nutrition, Paso del Norte Health Foundation).

## 7. Expected Outcomes and Impact

1. The first characterization of how dairy-inclusive dietary patterns modulate cardiometabolic risk across the menopause transition in Latina women, with the phenotypes and biomarkers that explain who responds.
2. A validated, field-deployable precision model (and minimal biomarker panel) predicting cardiometabolic response to dairy-inclusive patterns by menopausal stage and biological profile.
3. Pilot evidence that phenotype-guided, culturally-tailored dairy-inclusive guidance outperforms standard guidance on cardiometabolic and musculoskeletal outcomes.
4. Actionable, equity-centered inputs to personalized nutrition guidance for peri- and menopausal women — advancing NDC's priority of understanding dairy's role *within* dietary patterns, and positioning a competitive follow-on (e.g., NIH R01) full trial.
