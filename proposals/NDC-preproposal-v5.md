## TITLE  [limit: 150 chars incl. spaces]

Precision Nutrition with Dairy in Midlife: Dairy Patterns, Metabolic Phenotype, and Menopausal Stage in Border Latina Women

**Character count (with spaces): 123 / 150 — 27 remaining**

---

## BACKGROUND / INTRODUCTION  [limit: 1500 chars incl. spaces]

Latina women in the U.S.–Mexico border region carry a disproportionate cardiometabolic burden; our El Paso community cohort documents metabolic syndrome (MetS) prevalence near 37%, among the highest reported for any U.S. Latino population. The menopause transition compounds this through shifts in lipid metabolism, adiposity redistribution, insulin resistance, blood pressure, and accelerating bone and muscle loss. Nutrient-rich dairy foods supply calcium, potassium, protein, and bioactive peptides relevant to these pathways, and observational evidence links dairy-inclusive patterns to more favorable cardiometabolic profiles. That evidence derives largely from non-Hispanic White cohorts, reports small average associations once diet quality and adiposity are controlled, and rarely resolves which women, at which stage, consuming which formats, benefit. Signals concentrate in fermented and unsweetened products and in metabolically vulnerable subgroups rather than total intake. Interindividual variability, not the average effect, is the precision-nutrition question, and midlife Latina women remain critically underrepresented in it. Anchored in an established community screening and biospecimen platform, this study characterizes that variability—by dairy product type, metabolic and hormonal phenotype, and menopausal stage—with a nested repeated-measures component to begin resolving short-term response, informing personalized, culturally relevant guidance.

## HYPOTHESES  [limit: 750 chars incl. spaces]

H1: Total dairy intake is not uniformly associated with cardiometabolic profile; fermented and unsweetened dairy-inclusive patterns are more favorably associated with metabolic syndrome and its components (waist circumference, triglycerides, HDL-cholesterol, blood pressure, glycemia) than total or sweetened dairy, particularly in metabolically vulnerable women. H2: Metabolic phenotype, hormonal/menopausal stage, and metabolomic signature modify dairy–cardiometabolic and dairy–musculoskeletal associations, identifying subgroups with the strongest favorable associations. H3: In a nested substudy, short-term cardiometabolic response to a dairy-inclusive pattern differs by baseline metabolic and metabolomic phenotype.

## SPECIFIC AIMS  [limit: 750 chars incl. spaces]

Aim 1: Characterize dairy-inclusive dietary patterns by product type (fermented, unsweetened, fat level, sweetened) across pre-, peri-, and postmenopausal stages in ~600 border Latina women; assess barriers to nutrient-rich dairy intake; stage menopause objectively (STRAW+10 plus DBS FSH/estradiol); and establish a dried blood spot (DBS) biorepository with metabolomic and microbiome profiling on a defined subsample. Aim 2: Estimate associations of dairy-inclusive patterns with cardiometabolic and musculoskeletal outcomes across menopausal stages. Aim 3: Determine whether metabolic, hormonal, and metabolomic phenotype and stage modify these associations, and in a nested substudy characterize short-term cardiometabolic response by phenotype.

*Note: Aim 3 is the precision-nutrition core the RFA most rewards (who benefits, under what conditions) — now strengthened with an in-study response measure rather than pure cross-section.*

## EXPERIMENTAL APPROACH  [limit: 2500 chars incl. spaces]

Design: Cross-sectional observational study with a nested repeated-measures substudy, enrolling ~600 women (ages 40–65) from the El Paso/Paso del Norte border region via the CHIP community screening platform at UTHealth Houston.
Recruitment: Community health worker (CHW) network at community sites, faith-based organizations, and health fairs. Eligibility: self-identified female, ages 40–65, border resident. Exclusion: pregnancy, insulin-requiring diabetes, prior cardiovascular surgery.
Menopausal staging: STRAW+10 from menstrual history and a validated symptom questionnaire, corroborated by DBS FSH and estradiol.
Dietary assessment: Validated food frequency questionnaire with a dairy module capturing product type, fat level, sweetened status, and fermentation; dairy-inclusive patterns derived by principal component analysis; overall diet quality scored. A brief implementation-science module assesses barriers (lactose-tolerance perception, affordability, availability, cultural fit, trusted sources).
Biospecimens: DBS collected by finger-prick and banked at -80°C for all participants. Untargeted metabolomics and gut-microbiome profiling on a defined subsample; odd-chain fatty acids C15:0/C17:0 quantified to objectively corroborate dairy-fat exposure.
Outcomes (measured in-study): blood pressure, fasting lipids, fasting glucose and HbA1c, waist circumference (MetS per NCEP-ATP III); musculoskeletal function via grip strength and the Short Physical Performance Battery.
Nested substudy: a phenotype-stratified subsample (~60) completes a standardized dairy-inclusive dietary challenge with repeated pre/post cardiometabolic measures to characterize short-term response.
Covariates: age, BMI, physical activity, acculturation, energy intake, smoking, alcohol, socioeconomic status.
Analysis: stage-stratified logistic and linear regression; moderation tests for metabolic, hormonal, and metabolomic phenotype with FDR correction; mixed-effects models for the repeated-measures substudy. The design can return null or unfavorable associations. Target N supports detection of moderate main effects across three strata; interaction and substudy analyses are powered for effect estimation to guide a subsequent trial.

## CONTRIBUTION TO THE DAIRY INDUSTRY  [limit: 750 chars incl. spaces]

This study will deliver the first biomarker-supported characterization of dairy-inclusive dietary patterns and cardiometabolic and musculoskeletal profiles across menopausal stages in U.S.–Mexico border Latina women, a large and understudied population. By resolving dairy product type, metabolic and metabolomic phenotype, and menopausal stage rather than total intake, and adding a nested response component, it identifies which women and which dairy patterns are associated with better profiles, and the barriers limiting beneficial intake. The DBS biorepository, metabolomic/microbiome data, and barriers module create a foundation for dairy-inclusive intervention trials and for personalized, culturally relevant guidance.
